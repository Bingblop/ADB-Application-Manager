  5. Tap Pause. Scroll to the end and look for red E or F rows. Type AndroidRuntime in the filter box and press Enter to see just the crash. A crash in native code shows "Fatal signal" instead.
  6. Read the lines. A row FATAL EXCEPTION starts the report. The line Process: names the app. The next line names the error, for example a null pointer. A Caused by: line gives the real cause. Lines starting with at say where in the code it happened.
  7. Tap Share and send the text to the developer, or tap Save: the file goes to Download/ADB App Manager as logcat_all_date_time.txt (with the app's package name when one app is picked).
Careful. A log can hold personal details: account names, addresses, the names of your apps. Read it before you share it, and filter to one app first. Clear cannot be undone, but it only empties the log, not your apps. For a Wear OS watch use Read a watch's log.

What you need

  • A working mode for commands such as pm and dumpsys. Without one, the shell This app (sandbox) still runs file and text commands.

Steps

  1. Open Command-Line Interface and tap Terminal. A black screen prints a welcome.
  2. Next to Shell, choose Working mode (ADB, Shizuku, Root). The screen prints a line such as "sh ready through ADB, running as the shell user (uid 2000)". (A uid is the account number Android runs the command under. 2000 is the shell user: it can do a lot, but it is not root.)

At the end, the cache of every app on your phone has been cleared in one go, and you have seen how much free space it gave back. No app, setting, login or file is deleted.

What you need

  • A working mode that is ready: the badge at the top right is green or violet. Without one, the button shows a padlock and tapping it opens a sheet called Working Mode Required.
  • A minute or two. The app stays usable while it works.
  • To know what a cache is: temporary files that an app keeps to save time, such as downloaded thumbnails, web pages a browser kept, map tiles and video that was only buffered. Apps make them again when they need them (see cache in the glossary).

Steps

  1. Open the SD Maid tab and find the card Trim Caches.
  2. Tap Trim Caches in All Applications. (The text under the button says what is trimmed and what is not.)
  3. A question opens: "Trim the caches of every app now? Only cache files are deleted. Apps, their data, logins and your files stay as they are." Tap OK. Cancel does nothing.
  4. The button turns grey and reads "Trimming caches…". A line under it says this can take up to a minute or two. You can keep using the app.
  5. A box titled Trim Caches in All Applications opens. Caches trimmed with SUCCESS means it worked, and a line such as "Free space on the phone: 120 GB before, 123 GB after (2.4 GB freed)." tells you the result. If nothing more could be freed, the line ends "(nothing more could be freed)" instead. Close the box.

If the box says It did not finish with FAILED, Android refused and the words under it are its own. See Troubleshooting, "Trim Caches says it did not finish".

What to expect

  • The button runs pm trim-caches 128G, the same as adb shell pm trim-caches 128G on a computer. It asks Android to delete app caches until the phone has 128 GB free. No phone has that much free space, so the target is never reached, and Android clears every cache it is allowed to clear, in every app. The order is Android's own choice.
  • How much it frees depends on how much cache your apps hold. It can be a little or a lot, and the box shows the free space before and after.
  • The first start of an app afterwards can be a little slower, and it may use mobile data to fetch images again.
  • An app that keeps offline downloads in its cache folder (some music, video and map apps do) may need to download them again.
  • Apps that are running right now may keep some of their cache until they close.

What it cannot do

  • It does not delete files, downloads, photos, videos or app data, and it uninstalls nothing. So it frees only what caches took up.
  • For big amounts of space, find what is large and decide: Find the apps that take the most space.
Undo. There is no undo: a cache that was cleared cannot be put back. That is safe, because apps make it again by themselves, but an app with offline music, videos or maps in its cache may need to download them again. If that worries you, do it on Wi-Fi.
