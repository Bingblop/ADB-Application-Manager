Android keeps hundreds of small settings that its own Settings app never shows. This tab lists them, lets you read any one, change it, add your own or delete it, and puts the old value back when you change your mind.

Where to find it: the tab named Hidden Settings (two lines on the tab bar: Hidden and Settings), between Command-Line Interface and RRO/Monet Customization in the default order. If it is missing, it has been switched off: see Turning tabs on and off and moving them.

Before you start

  • You need a working mode. The app reads and changes these settings by running Android's own settings command through your working mode (ADB over TCP, Wireless Debugging, Shizuku or Root; see Working modes and the glossary). In Read-Only mode the tab cannot even show the list. With no mode ready, a card says Needs ADB, Wireless Debugging, Shizuku or Root and its Set Up button opens Working Modes. Connect one, come back, and the list loads.
  • No computer is needed, and the tab does not use the app's own "Write Secure Settings" permission (see App permissions). What you can change is decided by the mode and by Android.
  • Which settings exist depends on your Android version and phone maker. One your phone does not have is not in the list.
Risky. These are the settings Android reads while it runs. A wrong value can cut the connection this app uses, lock you out of the keyboard or screen, or upset the system. The app marks the worst ones careful and asks first, but others can also cause trouble. If you do not know what a setting does, leave it alone. Before you change one, tap its row and use Copy value to keep the original in a note. Every change you make can be put back (see Undoing settings changes), but a lock-out can stop you reaching the app, so read "If you lose the connection" below first.

The three tables

A setting is a name with a value, such as screen_off_timeout with 60000. Android keeps them in three groups called tables, and the tab has one sub-tab for each.

TableWhat lives thereExamples
GlobalSettings for the whole phone.adb_enabled, airplane_mode_on, window_animation_scale, device_name
SecureSettings of the current user that only the system, or the shell, may change: keyboards, accessibility, location mode.default_input_method, location_mode, ui_night_mode
SystemThe classic per-user settings: brightness, screen timeout, text size, rotation, volumes.screen_off_timeout, font_scale, volume_music

Android reads many settings only at certain moments, and some phone makers overwrite a few with their own, so a change that "does nothing" is not always a fault.

What the screen shows

  • Global, Secure, System (three wide buttons) choose the table. The number under each name is its count of settings: an ellipsis while loading, an exclamation mark if it could not be read, and "matches / total" while a search or filter is on.
  • Search settings... (the box, with an x to clear it), ＋ (create a setting) and ⋯ (More: sort order, reload, your changes, copy and share).
  • Four chips: All; Switches (values such as 1 or 0, true or false, on or off, yes or no); Described (every setting the app can describe: about 1,100 of Android's own; see "What a setting does and what its values mean" below); Edited (the ones you changed with this app).
  • A status line: the count, the time it was read, and a Refresh link.
  • The list. Each row has the name, what the setting does and which values it takes when the app knows them, and the value (long values are cut after two lines). A dot after the name means you changed it. An orange edge and the word "careful" mark a risky one. (empty) means nothing is stored, (blank) only spaces. A switch at the right means the value is switch-like. The list shows 120 rows at a time; more load as you scroll or with Show more.

What a setting does and what its values mean

Under the name of each setting the list says what it does, and below that Values: which values it takes. The value the setting holds now is in bold in that line, so Values: 0 = off; 1 = priority interruptions only; 2 = total silence; 3 = alarms only tells you at a glance that Do Not Disturb is off. Tap a row and the sheet has an About box with the same, one value to a line, and a Now: line that follows what you type (type 2 and it says Now: 2 = total silence).

  • Where the text comes from. The app knows what about 1,100 of Android's own settings do (the Global, Secure and System tables of Android's source code). The ones people meet most often have been written out in plain words; the others carry the short text from Android's documentation.
  • Reading the values. 0 = off; 1 = on is a switch. A time is in milliseconds (1000 is one second, 60000 one minute). A decimal such as 1.0 is a scale: 1.0 normal, 0.5 half, 2.0 double. percent, Hz (refresh rate) and kelvin (colour warmth) are units. A list uses , or : between its items; package/class is an app component.
  • Only real switches flip. A setting the app knows takes more than 0 and 1 (for example wifi_on, zen_mode) is never flipped by a press and hold: tap it and pick one of its values.
  • The search also looks in the values text, so milliseconds or total silence finds settings.
  • A setting without a description. Phone makers and apps add many settings of their own (a name starting sec_ often comes from Samsung, miui_ from Xiaomi, a name like com.example.app.key from that app). For these the editor says What it probably is and guesses from the name and the value: an on/off switch, a time in milliseconds, a web address, a package name. It is only a guess; if you cannot tell what a setting does, leave it alone.
  • The How to read this list link under the title of the tab opens a short explanation of the three tables, a row and the kinds of value. The ? next to the title opens this guide topic.

Find a setting

  1. Open Hidden Settings. Grey bars show while the tables are read; then each button shows its count.
  2. Tap a table, or just search: the counts on all three buttons change to "matches / total".
  3. Type in the search box. It looks in the name, the value and the description, ignores capitals, and every word you type must match. timeout finds screen_off_timeout.
  4. Narrow the list with a chip, or tap ⋯ and choose Sort by: Name A-Z (default), Name Z-A or Value.

What you will see: the list shrinks to the matches, with the matching letters highlighted. If nothing matches, the page says Nothing matches "text" in Global. and offers Create "text" in Global (only when what you typed is one word that can be a setting name) and Show everything. Back clears the search first. The table, filter and sort order you used last are remembered; the search text is not.

Read a value

The value is on the row. To see all of it, tap the row: a sheet opens with the value in a box. The app also asks the phone for the real value while it opens, because the list may be a little old; if it differs, the box is updated and a message says Showing the value the phone holds now. The sheet's Reload link does the same on demand.

Change a value

  1. Tap the row. A sheet slides up with the name, the table, the description and, for a risky setting, an orange box explaining the danger. Read it.
  2. Type in the Value box, or tap a quick value. If the setting is documented, its own values come first, each with its meaning (for example 2 · total silence); then 0, 1, true, false, null, (empty), -1. A line under the box says what kind of value it looks like and what it was before.
  3. A red line means it cannot be saved yet. The limits are 20,000 characters, no control characters (invisible special codes, apart from a tab or a line break), and a size that fits in one command sent through ADB (about 62,000 bytes once quoted). Save stays grey until the problem is gone.
  4. Tap Save. For a careful setting your phone first asks you to confirm.

What you will see: the sheet stays open while the phone applies the change, so a refusal never loses what you typed. On success it closes, the row shows the new value with a dot, and an Undo bar appears for about 7 seconds. Saving the same value says No change. The word null is stored as that text; use (empty) for nothing and Delete to remove the setting. The sheet's other links: Web search (looks the setting up on the web by its table and name), Explain (AI) (asks the Coding Agent you chose in the Command-Line Interface tab what the setting is and whether it is safe to change; the setting's name, table and current value are sent to that agent's company, only when you tap the button, and many settings belong to the phone maker, so the answer may be a careful guess), Copy name, Copy value and Copy command (the exact settings put line, to paste in the ADB Console pane of the Command-Line Interface tab).

Flip a switch

  1. Tap the switch at the end of a row, or press and hold the row for about half a second (you feel a short tap).
  2. Confirm if it is a careful setting.
  3. The switch moves and the Undo bar says, for example, show_touches: 0 → 1.

Capitals are kept (True becomes False). Settings that look like switches but are really a choice among several values, such as zen_mode, ui_night_mode or screen_off_timeout, have no switch (holding one says Not a switch: tap it to edit). In the editor, a switch-like setting has a Flip button that only changes the box; you still tap Save.

Add a new setting

Android uses a setting only if something reads it by that exact name, so an invented one does nothing. Add settings you have a real name for, from a guide or an app's documentation.

  1. Tap ＋. A sheet titled New setting opens.
  2. Choose Global, Secure or System (it starts on the table you were in).
  3. Type the Name: up to 256 characters, not starting with a dash, with no equals sign, spaces or control characters. A red line says what is wrong.
  4. Type the Value, or leave it empty to create the setting with nothing in it.
  5. Tap ＋ Create. If the name exists, the button reads Replace, a note shows the current value, and you must confirm. The app asks the phone first, so a name missing from an old list is still caught.

What you will see: the list jumps to that table with the name in the search box, and the Undo bar says "＋ Created name". Undo deletes it again.

Delete a setting

  1. Tap the row, then Delete (red, at the bottom of the sheet).
  2. Confirm. The box says Android and apps then behave as if it was never set.
  3. The row disappears and the Undo bar says "Deleted name". Undo brings it back with its old value.

Twelve settings carry the warning. The app asks before every change, flip, delete or revert of them.

The ⋯ button

The sheet Hidden settings tools has Sort by, Reload all, Changes (your history; see Undoing settings changes), Copy shown and Share shown (the rows currently shown, one name=value per line, by copy or by the share sheet), plus a short value guide. The lists can hold personal details such as the device name, Bluetooth address and Android ID, so do not post one without reading it.

If you lose the connection

If you switched off adb_enabled or adb_wifi_enabled (or Wi-Fi, or switched on airplane mode) while the app was using ADB, the app can no longer reach the phone to change them back. Open your phone's own Settings, go to Developer options (a hidden part of Android's Settings; if you cannot find it, search the Settings for its name), switch USB debugging or Wireless debugging back on (and Wi-Fi), then reconnect in Working Modes. If another mode (Shizuku or Root) still works, Revert the entry in Changes instead. If a keyboard or accessibility setting locks you out, use your phone's own Settings to choose a working keyboard and switch accessibility services back, then revert the entry.

Hidden Settings keeps two ways back: an Undo bar that appears right after a change, and a history called Changes where every change you made can be reverted later.

Where to find it: the Undo bar appears at the bottom of the screen in the Hidden Settings and RRO/Monet Customization tabs. The history is under the ⋯ button of Hidden Settings, in the Changes button.

Before you start

  • Putting a value back is a change like any other, so it needs a working mode (see Working modes).
  • The history only knows about changes made with this app. If another app, Android itself or a computer changed a setting, it is not listed, and the app cannot tell you what the old value was.
  • The page has no backup and no restore for settings. There is no button that saves a whole table to a file or loads one back. The history is the safety net, and the section "Keep your own copy" below shows a manual way to save a list before you experiment.

The Undo bar

After a change, a small bar slides up from the bottom of the screen. It shows a short line in a typewriter font and an Undo button. It stays for about 7 seconds and then slides away.

  1. Make a change.
  2. Within about 7 seconds, tap Undo on the bar.
  3. A message says Put back: name (or Removed: name when the setting did not exist before), and the row shows the old value again.

What you will see: the bar disappears when you tap Undo, when 7 seconds have passed, when a newer change takes its place (only the newest change has an Undo bar), or when you leave the Hidden Settings and RRO/Monet Customization tabs. After the bar is gone, use Changes.

Tip. If you change a theme and Android restarts the app (this happens when the colours change), the app comes back to the RRO/Monet tab by itself with the Undo bar waiting. This works for changes made in the last 20 seconds.

The Changes history

  1. Open Hidden Settings and tap ⋯ next to the search box.
  2. Tap Changes. A sheet titled Changes opens, with the line "Made with this app, newest first. Revert puts the old value back."
  3. Each entry shows the setting name, the table and how long ago (for example "Global · 3 min ago"), and the change as old value → new value. A value that was not there before shows as (not set); an empty one as (empty).
  4. Tap Revert under the entry you want to undo.

If nothing has been changed yet, the sheet says Nothing was changed with this app yet. The same history is behind the Edited filter chip and the small dot after the name of every changed setting in the list.

Revert one change

  1. In Changes, find the entry and tap Revert.
  2. If the setting has changed again since (by you, by Android, or by another app), a box says name has changed since: it is now ... (this change left it ...). Put the old value back anyway? Choose OK only if you want the old value back regardless.
  3. If the setting is marked careful, you are asked to confirm once more (see Hidden Settings).
  4. The sheet closes and a message says Put back: name (or Removed: name when the entry was a setting you created, so there is nothing to put back).

Reverting is recorded as a new change, so it appears at the top of the history and can itself be reverted.

If the old value was longer than 2,000 characters, the entry shows (too long to keep) in its place and has no Revert button, because the old text was not stored. For those, use the Undo bar right after the change, or fix the setting by hand.

Revert many changes

There is no "revert all" button. Revert the entries one at a time, starting from the top (the newest), so that every entry finds the setting in the state it left it. Going out of order is allowed, but you will get the "has changed since" question for entries that depend on a later one.

  1. Open ⋯ → Changes.
  2. Tap Revert on the first (newest) entry. The sheet closes after each revert.
  3. Open ⋯ → Changes again and repeat. Check your phone from time to time, so you stop as soon as it behaves normally.

What is kept, and where

  • The last 150 changes. Older ones are forgotten as new ones arrive.
  • Where: inside this app's own storage on the phone. The app does not write a file to Downloads or anywhere else, and nothing is sent to anyone. The history survives closing the app and restarting the phone. Because it lives in the app's own storage, clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it (and with it your way back).
  • Theme changes too. A Material You theme change made in the RRO/Monet tab is a change of the secure setting theme_customization_overlay_packages, and is listed here with that name. Reverting it puts the old theme setting back. Overlay on/off changes are not listed in Changes, only in the Undo bar.
  • What is in each entry: the table, the name, when it happened, the old value and the new value.

Clear the history

At the bottom of the Changes sheet, Clear history asks Forget the history of changes? The settings themselves stay as they are. If you confirm, the list is emptied, the small dots and the Edited filter forget everything, and nothing on the phone changes. You cannot get the entries back, so do this only when you are happy with how the phone behaves. The button only shows while there is something to clear.

Keep your own copy before you experiment

The history cannot restore a whole table, and it does not know settings you did not change. If you are about to try several things, save the original values first.

  1. Open Hidden Settings and choose the table (Global, Secure or System).
  2. Clear the search box and tap All, so that every setting is shown.
  3. Tap ⋯, then Copy shown (a message says Copied 340 settings or similar), and paste into a notes app. Or tap Share shown and send the text to yourself.
  4. Do the same for the other tables if you want to.

The copy is plain text with one name=value line per setting. To restore something later, find the line and set the value by hand (tap the row, type, Save), or create the setting with ＋ if it was deleted. The app cannot read that text back in for you.

Careful. The lists can hold personal details, such as the device name, the Bluetooth name and address, and the Android ID. Keep the copy private. If a very long list does not go through the share sheet, narrow it with the search or a filter first.

If something goes wrong

  • Revert says it could not change the setting. A sheet shows Android's own answer, the same as for any change. Read it; see "If a change is refused or nothing happens" in Hidden Settings.
  • The history is empty but you know you changed things. The changes were made outside this app, or the app's data was cleared since. Set the values back by hand from your own copy.
  • The phone is unusable after a change and you cannot reach the app. Use your phone's own Settings (Developer options, language and input, accessibility) to undo what you can, reconnect in Working Modes, then revert the entry. See also Safety.

Change the colours Android uses for its own screens and for apps that follow it (the "Material You" theme), and switch the system's look-changing add-ons, called overlays, on or off.

Where to find it: the tab named RRO/Monet Customization (two lines on the tab bar), right after Hidden Settings. If it is missing, it is switched off: see Turning tabs on and off and moving them.

Two words first

  • Overlay (RRO). RRO means Runtime Resource Overlay: a small package that changes the look of another app, or of Android itself (colours, icon shapes, fonts, the navigation bar), without touching that app. Each overlay is on or off. The app it changes is its target.
  • Material You, or "Monet". From Android 12, Android builds all its system colours from one source colour: your wallpaper's, or one you choose. A style decides how vibrant the palette is. Google apps and apps that support Material You follow it. Monet is the name of that colour engine.

The tab has two halves, chosen with the two buttons under the title: Theme (with "Material You" under it) and Overlays (with the number of overlays under it).

Before you start

  • Changing anything needs a working mode (ADB over TCP, Wireless Debugging, Shizuku or Root; see Working modes). Without one, a card says Needs ADB, Wireless Debugging, Shizuku or Root with a Set Up button. The colours in use are still shown, because the app reads them from Android directly.
  • The Theme half needs Android 12 or newer. On Android 11 and older the tab opens on the Overlays half (unless you chose a half before), Apply theme and Default are greyed out, and the page says Material You needs Android 12 or newer.
  • Phone makers differ. The app writes the setting that Android's own wallpaper picker writes, so phones close to stock Android are the most likely to follow it. Some makers add their own palette on top (the page says so), and a few refuse changes from a shell. The app cannot tell in advance. Try one colour, and use Undo or Default if you do not like it.
  • Samsung. If the phone has Samsung's "wallpaper colours" switch (a setting called wallpapertheme_state), Apply theme also sets it to match (off for a colour you chose, on for the wallpaper) and reads it back; Undo restores it. If it will not change, a message says the phone's own palette may still win. The app does not talk to Samsung's theme store or to Good Lock (Samsung's own customisation app); it only writes Android's standard theme setting and, where it exists, that one switch.
  • On Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO a change may need USB debugging (Security settings) switched on in Developer options.
Careful. A theme change repaints the whole system and Android usually restarts apps, this one included. Save your work elsewhere first. The app notices the restart and brings you back to this tab with the Undo bar waiting. Overlays can also change the navigation bar or system interface, so the wrong one can make the screen harder to use. Every change here can be undone.

The Theme half

Colors in use

The top card shows what Android uses now: a swatch and a title, either System default, From your wallpaper (with the style), Custom color (with a code such as #6750A4 · Tonal Spot) or Another theme (set by something else; applying here replaces it), plus a time such as "2 min ago". Below are five rows of squares, Accent 1, Accent 2, Accent 3, Neutral 1 and Neutral 2, eleven shades each from light to dark. Tap a square to copy its code (Copied #6750A4 (Accent 1 500)). Refresh reads the colours again; after a change they redraw by themselves a few seconds later.

Choose the colour source
  1. Under Color, tap Wallpaper (Android builds the palette from your home-screen wallpaper, its default) or Custom color (from one colour you choose).
  2. With Custom color a box appears with a big square and a code field (a hex code is six letters and digits that name a colour). Pick the colour in one of the ways below.
  • Type the code: 6750A4, #6750a4, #abc or FF6750A4 (the first two digits, transparency, are dropped). A red line says Use six hex digits, like 6750A4 if it is invalid.
  • Move the sliders: Hue (0 to 359), Saturation and Lightness (0 to 100). The code and the square follow.
  • Tap a Recent colour: the last eight you applied.
  • Open the presets: tap Preset colors 657, search by name or code, narrow with the chips All, Neutral, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Teal, Blue, Purple, Pink, and tap a round swatch. Its name and code show under the grid. The grid shows 96 at a time with a Show more button. Tapping a preset also switches the source to Custom color.
Choose a style

Under Style, tap one of six options (a round button marks the active one):

Apply a colour scheme
  1. Choose the source, the colour (if custom) and the style.
  2. Read the line under the buttons: Android repaints in a moment, and apps may restart. If it says This is already what the phone uses., there is nothing to apply. With an invalid code, Apply theme stays grey.
  3. Tap Apply theme. It reads Applying... while the app reads the current setting, writes the new one and reads it back.
  4. Android repaints within a second or two and may restart the app. The Undo bar appears for about 7 seconds with Theme applied, and the colours in use update.

The app keeps other choices stored in the same setting (a font, icon shape or icon pack on Pixel-like phones) and replaces only the colour entries. It writes one setting, the secure setting theme_customization_overlay_packages, and lists the change in Changes in Hidden Settings, with a Revert. Default asks Go back to the system default?; if you confirm, the theme setting is cleared (wallpaper colours, Tonal Spot) and Samsung's switch is left alone. The How this works card has Copy the setting (the raw setting text, for a bug report) and Reload.

Tip. The app's own look can follow these colours: the Material You palette in Themes and colours uses them, so this app may change colour too.

The Overlays half

Tap Overlays. The page asks Android's overlay service for its list (what cmd overlay list prints) and shows it:

  • Search overlays... filters by name or target; Reload reads the list again. Chips: All, On, Off and Theme (names that look like theme, colour, icon pack, navigation bar, font or shape). Your chip is remembered.
  • A status line, such as 83 overlays · 21 on, the time read, and Refresh.
  • The list is in three parts, in this order: Enabled first, then Disabled, then Installed, not changeable (overlays Android lists but cannot switch), each with its count. Inside a part, overlays are grouped under their target (such as android or com.android.systemui) with a count; those with no target are under Other.
  • Each row shows the name, a very short description of what it is for (for example Gesture navigation: swipes instead of buttons), its state (On, Off or Unavailable) and a switch. The description is a best guess made from the overlay's name and target, so treat it as a hint, not a promise. Unavailable means Android lists it but it cannot be switched (its target app is missing, or it is not approved); the row is dimmed. 120 rows are drawn at a time.
Switch an overlay on or off
  1. Find the overlay (search or a chip).
  2. Tap its switch, or press and hold the row for about half a second (you feel a short tap).
  3. The app runs the change and reads the whole list again, so the row shows what Android reports, not what was asked for.
  4. The Undo bar says On: name or Off: name. Tap Undo within about 7 seconds to put it back.

The detail sheet. Tap a row (not the switch) to open a sheet with the full name, state and target, a note, Switch on or Switch off, and the links Copy name, Copy target and Copy command (the exact cmd overlay line).

When an overlay will not switch. Some overlays are fixed on or off by the system or belong to another app. Android 12 and newer answer with a bare "commit failed", and the app says so: Android refused the change and gave no reason. A sheet titled Could not switch it on (or off) shows Android's words and advice. If no answer comes in time, the phone may still apply it and the list is read again shortly. Overlays are switched for the user the phone's shell uses by default, which is the owner on nearly every phone.

If a colour looks bad: undo and recovery

  1. Tap Undo on the bar (about 7 seconds). The old theme is written back and a message says Theme put back.
  2. Or tap Default and confirm: wallpaper colours and Tonal Spot again.
  3. Or use the history: Hidden Settings, ⋯, Changes, find theme_customization_overlay_packages, Revert (see Undoing settings changes).
  4. Or delete the setting: in Hidden Settings open Secure, search theme_customization, tap the row and Delete. Android then uses its default.
  5. An overlay is the problem: in the Overlays half tap On, find it and switch it off.
  6. The screen is hard to use: use your phone's own wallpaper and style settings (if it has them) to choose a normal colour, restart the phone if the colours stay wrong, then revert the entry in Changes.

The app needs a working mode to write, so reconnect in Working Modes first if it dropped.

Each of the twelve movable tabs has a row with its name, an up arrow, a down arrow and a switch. The first row's up arrow and the last row's down arrow are greyed out because they have nowhere to go. A row whose switch is off has a dimmed name. This is the default order, which is also what Reset to Default gives you:

On the tab bar, long names break into two lines (for example Hidden / Settings). The names in the list are the full names.

Switch a tab off

  1. Tap the gear icon, then scroll to Feature List.
  2. Tap the switch at the right end of the tab's row, so it turns off. The tab's name in the list turns dim.
  3. Look at the tab bar under the header: the tab is gone. The tabs that were after it move up to take its place.

What happens to a tab you switch off:

  • It leaves the tab bar at once, and Back no longer goes to it.
  • Anything inside the app that points at it does not open it. A message says, for example, App Updater is turned off. Turn it on in Settings, Feature List. and nothing else happens: no sheet closes and no download starts. Examples are Check for update in About (App Updater), the update button in an app's menu (App Updater) and Logs in an app's menu (Logcat Viewer).
  • One exception: if you open an APK file from outside the app (for example from your file manager or a download, choosing this app to install it), the app switches APK Installer back on by itself, because you asked for it. A message says APK Installer was turned off. It is on again to open this file.
  • You can switch off every one of the twelve if you like. The bar then shows only Application Manager and About.

Get a tab back

  1. Open Settings with the gear icon and scroll to Feature List.
  2. Turn the tab's switch on. It returns to the bar in the position its row has in the list.
  3. If you cannot remember which tab you hid, or want everything as it was, tap Reset to Default.

Move a tab

  1. In the Feature List, tap the up arrow on a row to move that tab one place earlier, or the down arrow to move it one place later.
  2. The row moves and the tab bar follows at once. The screen scrolls so the row stays under your thumb, so you can tap the same arrow again to keep moving it.
  3. Repeat for other tabs until the order suits you.

A tab that is switched off still keeps its place in the list. If you switch it on again later, it returns to that place. Moving a tab never changes the position of Application Manager or About.

Reset to Default

The Reset to Default button under the list switches all twelve tabs on and puts them back in the default order shown above. A message says Tabs are back in their default order, all turned on. There is no question first, and it does not touch anything else in Settings.

When the app is updated

Your choice is checked against the tabs the new version has. A tab that no longer exists is dropped from your choice. A tab the update adds is placed after the tab that comes before it in the default order, so your saved order keeps working.

Tip. If you use only a few tabs, hide the rest and put the ones you use most right after Application Manager. The tab bar then shows more of what you need at once, without sideways scrolling.

If something goes wrong

  • "... is turned off. Turn it on in Settings, Feature List." You tried to open a tab that is off. Switch it on as described above.
  • A tab is missing and I do not know why. Open the Feature List: a row with a dimmed name and its switch off is a hidden tab.
  • I pressed an arrow and the row did not move. It is already first or last; its arrow is greyed out.
  • The order looks wrong after an update. Tap Reset to Default and arrange it again.

Choose how the app looks: light or dark, one of eight ready-made colour schemes, one of nine palette styles built from a source colour of your choice, or your own colours. These choices change only this app, not the rest of your phone.

Where to find it: tap the gear icon in the header to open Settings. Five cards are about looks: Appearance, Palette Style, My Themes, Curated Theme Palettes and, inside the same card below the palettes, Granular Color Pickers.

Before you start

  • No working mode, permission or computer is needed, and there is no Save button: each choice applies at once and is remembered.
  • The Material You palette needs Android 12 or newer. On older phones it falls back to the Material 3 baseline colours.
  • To change the colours of Android itself (the whole phone), see RRO/Monet Customization instead. This section is only about the app's own look.

How the app chooses its colours

Four things work together, in this order:

  1. The mode: light or dark. Appearance decides which one is in use now.
  2. The palette: one of the eight cards, or a palette style (Tonal Spot, Vibrant, Fidelity, Content, Neutral, Expressive, Fruit Salad, Rainbow or Monotone) built from the source colour. Either way there is a dark version and a light version.
  3. Your tweaks: any colours you changed with the colour pickers. They are saved for one mode only, so a tweak made in dark mode does not change light mode.
  4. Pure black: if it is on, then in dark mode the backgrounds become black whatever the palette and your tweaks say.

The text colour on buttons is chosen automatically, black or white, whichever is easier to read on the accent colour. The phone's status bar takes the colour of the app's background.

Light, dark or system

In the Appearance card, tap one of four buttons. The active one is highlighted, and a message says what you chose (for example Appearance: dark).

The eight palettes

Under Curated Theme Palettes ("Every palette has a light and a dark version") there are eight cards. Each card shows a name and a strip of four small swatches: the accent colour, the background, the card colour and the system-app colour. The swatches are a preview of how that palette looks in the mode you are in now, so they change when you switch between light and dark. They are not buttons of their own; tap the card.

  1. Tap a card, for example Midnight Slate.
  2. The app changes colour at once. The card is highlighted and a message says Applied Theme: Midnight Slate.
  3. Any colour tweaks you made before are cleared, for both light and dark. The palette's own colours are used.

My Themes

The My Themes card (below Palette Style) saves a look under a name: Save current theme…, then tap a saved theme to wear it again. It keeps the palette or palette style, the source colour, your colour tweaks and Pure black, and leaves Light, Dark and System alone. Each theme can be renamed, updated with the look in use now, or deleted. See Settings for every control.

Palette styles

The Palette Style card (above the eight palettes) builds a whole theme from one source colour in one of nine styles: Tonal Spot, Vibrant, Fidelity, Content, Neutral, Expressive, Fruit Salad, Rainbow and Monotone. The source colour is automatic (your wallpaper's main colour on Android 12 and newer, otherwise Material purple) until you pick one with the circle in the card. See Settings for what each style does and for the controls.

  1. Open Settings (the gear) and find Palette Style.
  2. Optional: tap the Source color circle and choose a colour, for example #009688.
  3. Tap a style. The app changes colour at once, the tile is highlighted and a message says Palette style: ....
  4. Switch Appearance between Light and Dark to see both versions. Tap one of the eight palette cards to leave the style palette.

Material You

Material You is Android 12's way of building the phone's colours from one source colour, normally your wallpaper. When you choose this palette, the app borrows the phone's accent and neutral colours, so it matches the rest of the phone.

  • When you change your wallpaper, or come back to the app after changing the system colours, the app reads the colours again and updates itself.
  • If you change the phone's colours with the RRO/Monet Customization tab, this app's Material You palette follows them too. Pick another palette if you want the app to keep its own colours regardless.
  • On a phone older than Android 12 the card's badge says NEEDS ANDROID 12+ • USES M3 BASELINE, and choosing it gives the Material 3 baseline with the message Material You needs Android 12+. Using Material 3 baseline.

The Granular Color Pickers change single colours of the palette that is in use. The card says Fine-tune the current palette. Tweaks apply to the current mode (light or dark). Tap a picker to open the colour popup (a grid of colours, or Custom with hue, saturation and brightness sliders, and a hex box that starts with a # and takes capitals only; see Themes and colours).

Undo a colour change and reset

  • Reset tweaks removes your tweaks for the mode that is showing. A message says Color tweaks reset. The other mode's tweaks stay.
  • Tap a palette card (even the one already in use) to clear the tweaks for both modes.
  • There is no single "reset everything" button. To return to the starting point, set Appearance to System, tap the Material You card (this also leaves a palette style), switch Pure black in dark mode off and, if you used it, leave Schedule at its default times 07:00 and 19:00.

Tips

Tip. Pick the accent first, then the background. Keep the background and card colours clearly different from the text colour. Very light text on a very light background is hard to read.
Careful. It is possible to pick colours that are hard to read, for example text-coloured cards on a similar background. Nothing is lost: tap Reset tweaks (top right of the pickers card), or tap any palette card to clear all tweaks. The gear icon in the header always stays reachable.
