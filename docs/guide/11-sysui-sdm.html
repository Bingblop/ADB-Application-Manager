Clean up what is not needed any more: SystemCleaner removes superfluous files, AppCleaner the expendable data of apps (caches and the like), CorpseFinder the leftovers of apps that are gone, and Deduplicator files that exist twice. Scan, look at the list, take out what you want to keep, then delete.

The tools are a port of the four tools of SD Maid SE by darken (GPL-3.0): the filters, the rules, the expendables databases and the automation steps are from there. This is not the SD Maid app, and it has none of its icons, logo or translations. If you want everything else SD Maid can do, the buttons in the card open SD Maid SE on GitHub and on Google Play.

Where to find it: the tab bar, after Morphe Patcher. If it is not there, it has been switched off in the Feature List.

Deleting is final. There is no trash and no way back for what a tool deletes, except what you restore from your own backup. Always look at the list first, and exclude what matters (see Exclusions).

The top of the tab

  • The working mode, and the data areas this phone lets the tools read: public storage, public app media, public app data, public app resources, private app data and portable storage. An area marked working mode is read through ADB, Shizuku or Root; one marked not available is skipped, and the hint under your finger says what it needs. Android 11 and newer hide the other apps' folders in Android/data from normal apps, and private app data needs Root, so the more privileged your working mode is, the more the tools can find.
  • Scan all starts every tool. Delete all results deletes what every tool found, after one question. Cancel all shows up while something works.
  • History, Exclusions and Settings open the pages described below.

Two at a time. At most two tools work at the same time; a third one waits and says In queue. The live status that stays at the top while anything works shows every tool that is working, its step, a bar and its path, and how many of the two places are in use.

Tip. The work goes on in the app's own process while you look at another tab. The results of a scan stay in memory, so rotating the phone or changing the theme does not lose them; closing the app does.

Tap Details on a card to see what a scan found, and take out whatever you want to keep.

  1. The first list shows the groups: the filters (SystemCleaner), the apps (AppCleaner), the remnants (CorpseFinder) or the sets of identical files (Deduplicator), each with its size. Every group starts ticked. Untick a group to leave all of it out. All and None tick or untick the whole list, and the search box narrows it.
  2. Tap a group to see what is inside: every path with its size. Untick single items. In Deduplicator the copy that is kept is marked Kept and cannot be ticked; the others are the copies that will be deleted.
  3. Tap Delete selected. The question says how many items and how much space, or, when nothing was taken out, the question of that tool ("Delete all app remnants?").

Exclude on a row is the lasting version of unticking: it adds an exclusion, takes the row out of the list at once and shows a bar with Undo. An app is excluded for AppCleaner as a whole; everything else by its path. What you untick is only remembered until the next scan or delete.

Before anything is deleted, every path is looked at again, because a scan can be hours old. A path that is already gone counts as deleted. A path that is not inside a data area, or that is a data area itself, is never deleted.

What each tool does

  • SystemCleaner walks storage once with its filters (log files, trash, thumbnails, empty folders, macOS and Windows leftovers, lost-and-found, temporary files, advertisements, analytics, usage and more) and groups what matches by filter. The filters you switch off in its settings are not used.
  • AppCleaner finds the caches and other expendable data of every app, by the rules SD Maid ships (browser caches, thumbnails, logs, code caches, and the app-specific rules for many popular apps). It also sees the cache an app keeps in its private folder, as far as Android shows its size; the caches no file access can reach are cleared with a working mode (all apps at once) or with the accessibility service (one app after the other).
  • CorpseFinder finds the folders and files of apps that are no longer installed, using the owner of each folder and a database of known leftovers. Each remnant shows how risky it is to delete.
  • Deduplicator finds files with identical content: it groups by size, compares a first block, then the SHA-256 of the whole file. Small files below the minimum size are not looked at.

An exclusion keeps an app, a file or a folder out of the tools you choose, so it is never listed and never deleted.

Open it with Exclusions. The list shows every exclusion with the tools it applies to. The ones marked default come with the app and can be removed; Restore defaults brings them back.

KindWhat it matches
AppOne installed app. AppCleaner leaves its data alone; CorpseFinder does not call its folders remnants.
PathOne file or folder and everything inside it. Write the absolute path, for example /storage/emulated/0/DCIM.
SegmentA part of a path, like DCIM/Camera, wherever it is. Allow partial matches lets the first and last name be a part of a longer name; Ignore casing ignores capitals.

Affected tools chooses where it applies: All tools, or any of the four. A folder that contains an excluded item is not deleted either, so that the excluded item stays. Export copies all exclusions to the clipboard; Import reads them back (and the exclusion files of SD Maid SE).

Everything the tools delete is written down: which tool, when, how much, and the paths.

Open History. Each report shows the tool, the time, Done, Partial success (for example, a run that stopped because the screen turned off) or Failure, and what it says on the card afterwards. Tap a report to see the paths that were deleted. Scans are never written, because nothing is deleted by them. The top shows how much space has been freed in all.

Reports are kept for 30 days and the paths for 7 days; both can be changed in the general Settings (0 keeps no paths). Reset all deletes all reports and sets the totals to zero.

Every tool has its own settings behind the gear on its card, and the tab has general settings behind Settings. They are the settings of SD Maid SE, with its texts; a change is used by the next scan.

  • The filters of SystemCleaner and AppCleaner can be switched on and off one by one; some need Root, and say so.
  • CorpseFinder lets you choose which areas are searched and how careful it is with files that may belong to something.
  • Deduplicator has the minimum size and the kinds of files.
  • Dry run in the general settings scans and reports as usual but deletes nothing. It is the safe way to try a tool the first time.
  • Reset to defaults puts one screen back to what the app ships with.

The accessibility service

Only AppCleaner uses it. Android does not let an app clear the cache of other apps, so SD Maid, and this tab, press the button for you: the service opens the App info screen of each app, finds Storage and taps Clear cache, while a cover over the screen shows what it is doing, with a way to cancel. It stops when the screen turns off or locks. Nothing is read or sent anywhere.

  1. In the Accessibility service card, tap Enable the service. The card explains what it does and asks for your consent, then opens Android's accessibility settings. Turn the service on there.
  2. On Android 13 and newer, an app installed from a file may be blocked from using an accessibility service: open this app's App info, the three dots, Allow restricted settings, and try again.
  3. With a working mode you can also tap Enable with the working mode, which turns the service on for you.
  4. Once the service is on and you gave consent, AppCleaner's Delete uses it after you confirm.

Withdraw consent in the card stops AppCleaner from using it. The automation follows the screen texts of the system settings, in the languages SD Maid has tables for; on some phone makers' systems it can fail, and then it says so and the rest of the cleanup still works.

Change how the status bar, the quick settings, the notifications and the system bars look and behave, without writing a single setting. Everything here ends by itself when System UI or the phone restarts.

The commands, the Demo Mode form and the three Quick Settings tiles follow the System UI Tuner app (Tweaker) by Zachary Wander, which is MIT-licensed. Most of what Tweaker offers writes Global, Secure and System settings. Those are not repeated here: the Hidden Settings tab does that, and the tab says so and has a button to open it. The other cards are additions of this app, not from Tweaker.

Where to find it: the tab bar, after SD Maid SE. Everything but the tiles needs a working mode (ADB, Wireless Debugging, Shizuku or Root); without one the tab says so.
